Trail 02

Waterproof Ratings Explained

Waterproof and breathability numbers on a jacket label are useful once you know what they measure, and what they leave out.

  • Waypoints5
  • Reading time7 min
  • UpdatedNov 2026
Waypoint 1

Hydrostatic head

Waterproofing is tested by placing a column of water on the fabric and measuring how tall it can get, in millimetres, before water comes through.

  • Under 5,000 mm: water resistant, fine for showers.
  • 5,000 to 10,000 mm: rainproof in moderate rain.
  • 10,000 to 20,000 mm: waterproof in heavy rain.
  • Over 20,000 mm: built for storms and long, wet days.
Waypoint 2

Breathability

Breathability, often given in grams of water vapour per square metre per 24 hours, describes how much sweat vapour can escape. Around 10,000 suits walking; 20,000 or more suits running, climbing and ski touring, where you produce more heat.

Waypoint 3

Seams and zips

A fabric rating means little if water gets in elsewhere. Look for fully taped seams, water-resistant or covered zips and a stiff peak on the hood. Pockets, vents and cuffs are where most leaks start.

Waypoint 4

DWR: the outer finish

A durable water repellent finish makes rain bead and roll off. When it wears out, the face fabric soaks up water, the jacket stops breathing and it feels as if it is leaking. Washing and reproofing as the care label says restores it.

Waypoint 5

Choosing your numbers

Match the rating to the weather you meet most. City showers need much less than days on an exposed ridge in driving rain. If you sweat a lot, give breathability and good venting as much weight as the waterproof figure.
